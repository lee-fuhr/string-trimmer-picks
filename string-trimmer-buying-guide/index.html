How to choose the right string trimmer

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

For most homeowners in 2026, a brushless battery-powered string trimmer offers the best balance of convenience, quiet operation, and power, especially if you already own tools on a shared battery platform. Focus on a model with a high-capacity battery, a straight shaft for reach, and an easy-to-load trimmer head. If you have over an acre of thick brush, a lightweight gas trimmer still wins on raw run-time and sustained cutting power, but expect more maintenance and noise.

Gas vs electric: which is right for your yard?

Gas trimmers still dominate for raw, sustained power on large properties. They never run out of charge mid-job and chew through heavy brush without bogging down. The trade-offs are real: fuel mixing, engine maintenance, and noise that will have your neighbors waving. Modern four-stroke gas trimmers are cleaner and easier to start, but they&#8217;re also heavier and cost more upfront. Battery models have closed the gap considerably. A good 60V or 80V brushless trimmer will handle weekly trimming on most suburban lots and even some tougher weeds. The real advantage is grab-and-go simplicity: no pull cord, no gas, no winterizing. If you already own a popular battery system from brands like Ego, Ryobi, Greenworks, or DeWalt, you can buy a trimmer �tool only� at a budget-friendly price. For the average quarter-acre lot, battery is the smart, practical choice. For over an acre of dense blackberry or saplings, consider a straight-shaft gas model from Stihl, Husqvarna, or Echo. But the tipping point keeps moving: 2026 sees more high-capacity batteries that push runtime closer to a tank of gas.

What to look for in a battery-powered trimmer

Battery voltage alone doesn&#8217;t tell you how well a trimmer will cut. A high-voltage trimmer can spin more torque, but a well-designed brushless motor and a bigger battery amp-hour (Ah) rating matter just as much. Look for a trimmer that uses the same battery platform as your other outdoor tools so you share chargers and packs. That alone saves money and clutter. Pay attention to the trimmer head. Many budget trimmers use a bump-feed head that is easy to reload. More expensive models offer a speed-feed or rapid-load head that spools a new line without taking the head apart. For occasional use, bump-feed is fine. For frequent trimming, the time saved with a speed-feed head is worth the small premium. A straight shaft is preferred for reach under fences and shrubs. A curved shaft is lighter and better for edging but can be harder to control for tall users. Also check if the trimmer has a variable-speed trigger or a cruise-control lock for long trimming sessions. A telescoping shaft is a nice bonus if multiple people of different heights will use it.

Pro tips for getting the cleanest cut

Trim with the head angled so the line strikes the grass at a 45-60 degree angle. This lets the tip of the line do the cutting and prevents the guard from bouncing off the ground. Keep the line length moderate; a longer line flops and reduces efficiency. Most trimmers work best with a line diameter of 0.065 to 0.095 inches. Thicker line handles heavy weeds but requires a heavier trimmer and uses more battery power. Replace the line when it wears down instead of waiting until it snaps. Dull line rips, not cuts. For stubborn weeds, upgrade to a star or square profile line. These cut more aggressively than round line. Avoid using the trimmer like a scythe: sweep across the target, don’t beat it. A steady, smooth arc produces a cleaner edge and reduces strain on the motor. If your trimmer has an edging function, flip the head and walk along the sidewalk or bed edge. Many battery trimmers make edging easy because they are lighter. For a professional-looking border, cut a shallow trench first with the trimmer tilted, then finish with the vertical edging mode.

What weight and balance mean for your back

A heavy trimmer with a motor at the top can be surprisingly fatiguing. Look for a model with a balanced design where the battery sits low or the motor is near the handle. Many battery trimmers mount the battery at the rear or use a backpack battery system, which shifts weight off your arms and onto your shoulders. This makes a huge difference when trimming for more than 20 minutes. Gas trimmers are generally heavier, especially with steel driveshafts. Consider a model with a padded harness if you expect to use it for over an hour. The harness distributes weight across your shoulders and lets the trimmer float rather than hang from your arms. Test the handle ergonomics if possible. Most trimmers come with a &#8220;j-handle&#8221; or &#8220;bicycle handle.&#8221; J-handles are fine for level trimming; bicycle handles offer more control for detail work. Also check the trigger pull. Some trimmers have a long stiff trigger that becomes uncomfortable after a half hour.

Common string trimmer features that are actually worth paying for

Brushless motors are the single best upgrade. They run cooler, quieter, and more efficiently than brushed motors, and they almost never wear out. If you buy battery-powered, make sure it is brushless. You will get more cut time from the same battery. Variable-speed control is useful for delicate work around flowers or young trees. A simple two-speed switch is fine; a variable trigger gives you precise control. The permanent &#8220;cruise control&#8221; setting lets you hold a consistent speed without squeezing the trigger��a godsend on long runs. Easy-load heads are a time-saver. Look for a head that lets you wind line in seconds without tools. Some models like the DeWalt 60V FlexVolt and Ego Power+ line have loaders that are really intuitive. If you buy a gas trimmer, check that the head is interchangeable; many users upgrade to a Speed-Feed or T35 head. A straight shaft is worth the extra money for reach and versatility. Curved shafts are lighter but limit your ability to trim beneath shrubs. A detachable shaft that converts to a power head is only worth it if you plan to buy multiple attachments like a pole saw or edger.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a string trimmer to edge my lawn?

Yes, many battery trimmers have an adjustable or rotating head that flips to vertical for edging. Gas trimmers can also edge, though the weight makes it more tiring. For the neatest edge, hold the trimmer so the line spins away from the sidewalk to avoid blowing grass onto the pavement.

How often should I replace the trimmer line?

Replace it when the line wears down to about half its original length or gets a raggedy tip. For typical weekly trimming, a full spool might last a few sessions. Keep a spare spool handy so you don’t interrupt the job.

What is the best trimmer line thickness for thick weeds?

For dense weeds and brush, use 0.095-inch to 0.105-inch line. That thickness provides more mass to cut through thick stems. Just make sure your trimmer can handle it; the manual will list the maximum diameter. Thicker line also reduces runtime on battery models.

Do I need a trimmer with a harness for a large yard?

If you plan to trim for over an hour or have a heavy gas trimmer, a padded harness is highly recommended. It transfers weight from your arms to your shoulders and back, reducing fatigue. Many battery trimmers are light enough to go without, but a backpack battery system effectively provides a harness for the battery weight.

Should I get a gas or battery trimmer for a 1-acre property?

For one acre with mostly normal grass and light weeds, a battery trimmer with a 5.0Ah or larger battery can handle the job on one charge. If you have heavy brush or uneven terrain, a gas model still holds an advantage for raw power and instant refueling. The tipping point is how many batteries you are willing to buy and charge.

What is the difference between a curved and straight shaft trimmer?

Curved shafts are lighter and better for basic trimming of grass under fences. Straight shafts provide better reach for edging and cutting under shrubs, and they transfer power more efficiently for tough weeds. Most pros prefer straight shafts for their balance and versatility.

In shortFor 2026, the smartest buy depends on your lot size and how highly you value quiet, low-maintenance convenience. Battery trimmers are now excellent for most homes, especially if you stay within one battery ecosystem. Gas trimmers remain the workhorses for heavy brush and huge yards. Whichever you choose, focus on a comfortable balance, an easy-to-load head, and line diameter that matches your weeds. That combination will save you time and frustration every time you step into the yard.
